PIFRA Position Codes Explained: What Your Code Means and Where to Find It

Open any PIFRA salary slip and you will find a small forest of numbers: a personnel number, a DDO code, and a position code. Most government employees know their personnel number by heart and have at least heard of the DDO code. The position code is the quiet one — rarely explained, often confused with the other two, and the source of real headaches at transfer and promotion time. This guide explains what it is, where it lives, and how to make sure yours is correct.

What a position code actually identifies

A position code identifies your post — your designation and grade within the sanctioned strength of your department — rather than you as a person. Think of it this way: the government sanctions a certain number of posts (say, forty posts of Assistant in BPS-15 in a department). Each of those posts carries a position code. When you are appointed against one of them, that code becomes attached to your payroll record for as long as you hold that post.

This is the key difference from your personnel number, which follows you through your entire career. The position code follows the chair. Get promoted, transferred to a different post, or move to another office, and your position code changes while your personnel number stays the same. For a deeper reference on how codes are organized across the system, the PIFRA position code list guide collects the details in one place.

Position code vs DDO code vs personnel number

These three get mixed up constantly, so here is the clean separation:

CodeWhat it identifiesShared or personal?
Personnel numberYou, the employee, across your whole servicePersonal — stays with you through transfers and promotions
DDO codeYour office / cost centre (the Drawing and Disbursing Officer's jurisdiction)Shared — every employee in the same office carries the same DDO code
Position codeYour designation and grade — the specific sanctioned post you occupyPost-specific — changes when your post changes

The practical rule: if a form asks "who are you," that is the personnel number. If it asks "which office's budget does this belong to," that is the DDO code. If it asks "which post and grade," that is the position code.

Where the position code appears

You will meet your position code in a handful of places: printed on your monthly salary slip, stored in your service record, and carried inside the payroll data your DDO's office submits to the FABS system each month. It also surfaces in official correspondence about postings, transfers, and pay fixation — anywhere the administration needs to state exactly which post is being discussed.

Most employees never need to memorize it. But there are moments when having it right matters enormously, which brings us to the next section.

Why getting it right matters

Transfers. When you move offices, your payroll record must move from the old DDO's cost centre to the new one, and it must land against the correct position code at the new station. A record parked against the wrong code is one of the classic reasons the first salary after a transfer arrives late or short.

Promotions and upgradations. A promotion changes your grade, which means a new position code. If the payroll record is updated for the promotion but the position code field is left stale, the system may keep paying the old scale until someone notices.

Verifying your own record. Errors happen — a clerk keys one digit wrong, and your record points at someone else's post. A quick glance at the position code on your slip, compared against what your appointment letter says, catches this early. Corrections typically take several weeks to flow through the accounts office, so early is everything.

How to find your position code

The fastest route for most employees:

1. Check an old salary slip. The position code is printed on it. Any slip from the last few years will do.

2. Look it up on the portal. On the PIFRA portal, select your governmental sector, enter your DDO code, and search — your position code and related details are displayed against your record.

3. Ask your accounts office. Your DDO's accounts section maintains the sanctioned-post register and can confirm the exact code for your post in one lookup.

After any transfer or promotion: compare the position code on your next salary slip with the code in your posting/promotion order. If they do not match, raise it with your accounts office immediately — do not wait for the pay to look wrong first.

Common mistakes to avoid

Using the personnel number where the position code is asked. Forms in the payroll system treat these as different fields; swapping them is one of the most common data-entry errors.

Carrying an old code forward. After a transfer, some employees keep quoting the position code from their previous station. Always re-check after a move.

Guessing. Position codes are assigned by the system against sanctioned posts — there is nothing to work out by logic. If you do not know yours, look it up from an official record rather than inferring it.

Protect your data: Enter your CNIC, personnel number, and other personal details only on official .gov.pk portals. No guide site, agent, or social media page needs your CNIC to "verify" your position code.

Quick questions

Is the position code the same as my employee number? No. Your personnel (employee) number identifies you personally and never changes; the position code identifies your post and changes with transfers and promotions.

Can two employees share a position code? Only if they hold the identical sanctioned post, which is rare — in practice, treat it as identifying your specific post.

Who fixes a wrong position code? Your DDO's accounts office, coordinating with the district accounts office or AGPR. Bring your appointment/posting order as proof of the correct post.
